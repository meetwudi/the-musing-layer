# Ontology

The musing space needs two ontological layers:

1. A core ontology that captures what the system observes by default.
2. An evolved ontology that can grow on top of the core as the system learns what matters.

The evolved ontology should be allowed to change, but it should not grow without friction. AI likes adding categories. The system needs stabilization mechanisms so ontology growth reflects real use, not generative enthusiasm.

## Core Ontology

The core ontology should be small, durable, and product-shaping.

### Fragment

An intake unit from any channel.

### Person

A human participant with their own private and shared meanings.

### Perspective

The way a person or group relates to a shared object.

### Theme

A recurring area of attention.

### Question

An unresolved inquiry that remains alive over time.

### Project

A concrete or emerging body of work.

### Practice

A repeated way of acting, making, noticing, reflecting, or changing.

### Tension

A live contradiction, tradeoff, resistance, or unresolved pressure.

### Value

Something a person appears to care about, protect, return to, or refine.

### Pattern

A repeated structure in thought, action, relationship, or attention.

### Change

A detectable shift in framing, behavior, desire, taste, identity, or relation.

### Memory

A durable remembered structure with provenance and edit history.

### Observation

An AI-generated noticing about fragments, memories, or their relationships.

### Reflection

A synthesized view generated on request or through explicitly configured rituals.

## Evolved Ontology

The evolved ontology captures concepts that become locally meaningful over time.

Examples:

- A user's recurring personal phrase
- A team-specific concept
- A private distinction
- A named creative tension
- A way of classifying links
- A repeated failure mode
- A craft-specific pattern

## Stabilization

New ontology candidates should go through stages.

### Candidate

The system notices a possible new category or relation.

### Repeated

The candidate appears across multiple fragments, memories, or contexts.

### Useful

The candidate improves retrieval, reflection, editing, or human understanding.

### Named

A human or repeated usage gives the candidate a stable name.

### Stable

The candidate becomes part of the evolved ontology.

### Retired

The category no longer helps and should stop shaping retrieval or reflection.

## Anti-Sprawl Rules

- Do not create a new category when an existing one is sufficient.
- Prefer attaching a tag or note before adding a new ontology type.
- Require provenance for ontology changes.
- Track who or what introduced a concept.
- Track when a concept was last useful.
- Let humans merge, rename, suppress, or retire ontology nodes.
- Keep AI-suggested ontology separate from human-approved ontology.
